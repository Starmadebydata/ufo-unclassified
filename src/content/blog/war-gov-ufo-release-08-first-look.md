# PURSUE Release 08: A First Look at War.gov's Eighth UFO File Drop

The Department of War published [PURSUE Release 08](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4655441/department-of-war-publishes-eighth-release-of-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/) on November 20, 2026, the eighth batch of declassified and historical Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena files posted to [War.gov/UFO](https://www.war.gov/UFO/).

This is a first look, not a full analysis. It is built from the releasing office's own descriptions and metadata for each record rather than a page-by-page audit of every PDF, so every claim below is attributed to what the official descriptions state. The aim is to tell you what is actually in the batch and which records merit a closer read.

We have indexed all 43 records from this tranche: 40 PDFs and 3 military sensor videos totaling about 338 MB. After Release 07 shipped with no video or audio at all, Release 08 brings military video back into the archive. Only 3 of the 43 records are flagged as redacted in the official metadata, all three of them the new videos.

## What's in Release 08

Release 08 is larger than Release 07 (43 records versus 28) and much narrower in agency spread: every file comes from either the Department of War or the CIA.

| Source | Records | What they cover |
|---|---:|---|
| Department of War | 37 | One 2023 AARO radar analysis slide, three CENTCOM Middle East videos (2020, 2023, 2026), eight AAWSAP/BAASS records (2008-2010), seventeen 1956-1957 Air Intelligence Information Reports, and eight 1949-1966 California UAP reports |
| CIA | 6 | Six 1952 Office of Scientific Intelligence and senior leadership memos and diary entries on "flying saucers" |

Four distinct threads make up the batch: modern AARO/CENTCOM sensor material, the DIA-side paper trail on AAWSAP and Senator Harry Reid's Special Access Program request, the CIA's internal 1952 deliberations before the Robertson Panel, and a stack of 25 Cold War Air Force sighting files.

## An Unresolved 2023 Radar Track and Three CENTCOM Videos

The most operationally specific modern record in Release 08 is a single slide: [DOW-UAP-D165](/documents/release-08-file-003-dow-uap-d165-unresolved-radar-detection-north-america-2023/). It summarizes AARO's preliminary analysis of an unresolved 2023 radar event over the western United States.

According to the description, a civil air-surveillance radar system recorded eight detections over 84 seconds at an estimated altitude of 55,000 to 70,000 feet. If those eight detections belong to one continuous track, the cumulative point-to-point displacement is about 38.5 miles, giving a minimum mean horizontal ground speed of 1,650 mph, with an apparent 39-degree bearing change after roughly 48 seconds.

Read the caveat right beside the numbers. AARO explicitly states that the available data do not independently establish that all eight detections came from the same object or represented a continuous track, and that the data are insufficient for a full kinematic characterization. The case stays unresolved because the radar data are thin, not because AARO confirmed an extraordinary vehicle.

Alongside the radar slide, Release 08 adds three short CENTCOM Middle East sensor clips submitted to AARO, bringing the archive's video count to 119:

- [DOW-UAP-PR166](/documents/release-08-file-041-dow-uap-pr166-unresolved-uap-report-middle-east-2020/) (2020, 20 seconds): infrared footage tracking a single area of contrast moving right to left across the frame.
- [DOW-UAP-PR169](/documents/release-08-file-043-dow-uap-pr169-unresolved-uap-report-middle-east-2023/) (2023, 13 seconds): electro-optical/infrared footage panning to hold an area of contrast centered in frame.
- [DOW-UAP-PR168](/documents/release-08-file-042-dow-uap-pr168-unresolved-uap-report-middle-east-2026/) (2026, 54 seconds): electro-optical/infrared footage tracking multiple areas of contrast, zooming in on two while intermittently losing contact.

None of the three clips comes with an accompanying mission report in this batch, and all three carry redactions. Like earlier CENTCOM videos in the archive, they document sensor tracking events without giving enough telemetry to identify the targets on their own.

## Senator Harry Reid's SAP Request and DIA's Pushback on AAWSAP

Release 06 published the contracting framework for AAWSAP, and Release 07 added nine files from the contractor, Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS). Release 08 adds eight more AAWSAP records, and this time seven of them are from the Defense Intelligence Agency's side of the desk. That is what makes them the most revealing institutional thread in the batch.

- [DOW-UAP-D182](/documents/release-08-file-011-dow-uap-d182-aawsap-statement-of-work-august-2008/) (August 2008) is the formal Statement of Work establishing AAWSAP's 12 technical research areas out to a 2050 threat horizon.
- [DOW-UAP-D187](/documents/release-08-file-016-dow-uap-d187-dia-talking-paper-for-senator-reid-engagement-january-2009/) (January 2009) and [DOW-UAP-D184](/documents/release-08-file-013-dow-uap-d184-aawsap-talking-paper-for-senator-reid-engagement-may-2009/) (May 2009) are DIA talking papers preparing senior leadership for meetings with Senator Harry Reid. Both note that BAASS was the sole bidder on the August 2008 RFP, and the January paper already records a DIA assessment that the program produced reports of limited value and should be transitioned to another agency.
- [DOW-UAP-D183](/documents/release-08-file-012-dow-uap-d183-correspondence-from-senator-harry-reid-june-2009/) (June 2009) is Senator Reid's letter to Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn III asking that portions of AATIP/AAWSAP be placed into a restricted Special Access Program (SAP) with a bigoted-access list, asserting that the program had already produced sensitive unconventional findings. The official AARO comment notes that Reid used "AATIP" here as an alternate name for the same underlying AAWSAP effort.
- [DOW-UAP-D185](/documents/release-08-file-014-dow-uap-d185-review-of-aawsap-special-access-program-request-november-2009/) (November 2009) is DIA's formal rejection of Reid's SAP request. Per the description, DIA found no adequate justification: all first-year program documents were unclassified, the contractor had not established a secure facility during that period, much of the delivered material consisted of publicly available technical information, and DIA was unaware of any report sensitive enough to warrant heightened compartmentation.
- [DOW-UAP-D186](/documents/release-08-file-015-dow-uap-d186-dia-information-memorandum-on-aatip-aawsap-december-2010/) (December 2010) summarizes the whole arc through FY2010: the initial $10 million FY2008 appropriation (26 technical reports), a $12 million FY2010 appropriation (12 more reports), the rejected SAP request, and DIA's conclusion that the program offered limited value to the agency.
- [DOW-UAP-D172](/documents/release-08-file-004-dow-uap-d172-baass-ten-month-report-july-2009/) (July 2009) is the contractor's own ten-month report, summarizing technical papers, archival UAP research, and BAASS inquiries into parapsychological and paranormal claims at a Utah property.

Set side by side, D183 and D185 settle a long-running dispute over how DIA actually viewed AAWSAP's deliverables in real time. Senator Reid argued for a restricted SAP; DIA's internal review concluded the first-year outputs did not even meet the threshold for original classification.

## Six 1952 CIA Memos Leading Into the Robertson Panel

All six CIA files in Release 08 date to August through late 1952, the exact window between the July 1952 Washington sighting wave and the January 1953 [CIA Robertson Panel](/blog/cia-robertson-panel-1953-ufo-report/).

- [CIA-UAP-D026](/documents/release-08-file-009-cia-uap-d026-summary-assessment-of-flying-saucers-1952/) (August 1, 1952) and [CIA-UAP-D027](/documents/release-08-file-001-cia-uap-d027-office-of-scientific-intelligence-briefing-on-flying-saucers-1952/) (August 14, 1952) are Office of Scientific Intelligence assessments reviewing Air Technical Intelligence Center holdings: roughly 1,500 official reports since 1947, including 250 in July 1952, with about 20 percent listed as unexplained. After checking intelligence holdings and consulting ATIC, OSI initially recommended no further action.
- [CIA-UAP-D025](/documents/release-08-file-008-cia-uap-d025-proposal-to-consider-psychological-warfare-implications-of-ufo-related-issues-1952/) (October 2, 1952) is a draft memorandum from CIA Director Walter Bedell Smith proposing that the National Security Council examine the psychological warfare implications of UFO reports.
- [CIA-UAP-D028](/documents/release-08-file-010-cia-uap-d028-assessment-of-national-security-issues-relating-to-flying-saucers-1952/) (October 13, 1952) assesses "flying saucers" as a national security concern, and [CIA-UAP-D029](/documents/release-08-file-002-cia-uap-d029-official-diary-entry-on-flying-saucers-1952/) (October 28, 1952) records an internal diary discussion noting the difficulty of interpreting silent, non-flaring objects.
- [CIA-UAP-D024](/documents/release-08-file-007-cia-uap-d024-approval-in-principle-research-concerned-with-ufos-1952/) (late 1952) requests approval in principle to convene an external ad hoc panel of leading scientists. That is the direct administrative precursor to the Robertson Panel.

None of these six files overturns the established history of 1952 CIA UFO policy, but having them indexed together in PURSUE makes the paper trail from the July 1952 surge to the Robertson Panel easy to verify line by line.

## Twenty-Five Air Force Reports From 1949 to 1966

The bulk of Release 08 by count is 25 Cold War Air Force records: 17 Air Intelligence Information Reports (AIIRs) from 1956-1957 and 8 California Project Blue Book / Project 10073 files spanning 1949 to 1966.

Three stand out for their witnesses or documentation:

- [DOW-UAP-D206](/documents/release-08-file-033-dow-uap-d206-unresolved-uap-report-california-1953/) (December 16, 1953) is a complete digitization of reports submitted by Lockheed Chief Engineer Clarence "Kelly" Johnson and Lockheed flight-test personnel regarding a dark, elliptical or wing-like object observed near Agoura and Point Mugu, California. Even the releasing office's own blurb notes Johnson's reputation and familiarity with high-performance aircraft.
- [DOW-UAP-D190](/documents/release-08-file-005-dow-uap-d190-air-intelligence-information-report-massachusetts-1957/) (October 12, 1957) records two Air Force officers, two Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution vessel masters, and a civilian reporting a metallic cylindrical object 800 yards off Martha's Vineyard that changed direction twice and departed in roughly 20 seconds. The reporting office conducted no further investigation.
- [DOW-UAP-D197](/documents/release-08-file-006-dow-uap-d197-air-intelligence-information-report-michigan-1957/) (October 13, 1957) documents a reported silver-gray, bell-shaped object near Lake Erie, Michigan, estimated at 1,500 feet and 225 mph with a high-pitched humming sound.

The remaining AIIRs span Virginia, Texas, Nebraska, New Mexico, Washington, Japan, Iran, and Peru, mostly from October and November 1957. As in Release 07, the official descriptions carry a standard caveat that descriptive language reflects the observer's subjective interpretation at the time.

One metadata hygiene note for researchers pulling the raw war.gov CSV: six rows in Release 08 carry spreadsheet date-conversion artifacts in the official `Incident Date` column (`7/15/05` for 2023, `7/12/05` for 2020, `7/18/05` for 2026, `5/10/05` for 1957, and `12/10/26` for December 2010), and [DOW-UAP-D203](/documents/release-08-file-030-dow-uap-d203-air-intelligence-information-report-california-1957/) is titled "California, 1957" while its location field lists "Canada." We corrected the six garbled years in our index to match the document titles and descriptions.

## Common Questions About PURSUE Release 08

### How many files are in PURSUE Release 08?

Our index counts 43 records: 40 PDFs and 3 military sensor videos, totaling roughly 338 MB. That matches the 43 rows dated November 20, 2026 in the official War.gov data file.

### Which agencies are represented in Release 08?

Only two: the Department of War contributes 37 records and the CIA contributes 6. This is the most agency-concentrated batch since Release 06.

### What is the most significant file in Release 08?

For modern UAP policy and program history, the pair to read together is [DOW-UAP-D183](/documents/release-08-file-012-dow-uap-d183-correspondence-from-senator-harry-reid-june-2009/) (Senator Reid's 2009 SAP request) and [DOW-UAP-D185](/documents/release-08-file-014-dow-uap-d185-review-of-aawsap-special-access-program-request-november-2009/) (DIA's formal rejection). For recent sensor data, [DOW-UAP-D165](/documents/release-08-file-003-dow-uap-d165-unresolved-radar-detection-north-america-2023/) is the standout, with the caveat that AARO cannot confirm all eight radar detections belong to a single track.

### Is Release 08 the last PURSUE batch?

No. The Department of War states that files will continue to be released on a rolling basis as agencies finish declassification and review. No date has been announced for a ninth batch.

## What to Watch Next

Three follow-up reads from this batch deserve full page-by-page checks: comparing the text of DIA's November 2009 SAP rejection (D185) directly against BAASS's July 2009 ten-month report (D172); checking whether the 2023 civil radar detections in D165 overlap in time or geography with any earlier western U.S. records in Releases 03 and 07; and reviewing the full Kelly Johnson correspondence in D206 against the existing Project Blue Book historical record.

The bottom line is straightforward. Release 08 does not contain a smoking gun for exotic technology, and its three new CENTCOM clips arrive without mission reports or telemetry. What it does add is unusually clear institutional history: how CIA moved toward the Robertson Panel in late 1952, and why DIA declined to grant AAWSAP Special Access Program status in 2009.
